UX Research

Mapping pain points in the medical record

Structured research that analyzed 245 feedback entries to identify users’ main frustrations and guide product decisions.

Context and challenge

The medical record kept receiving negative feedback, but there was no structured view of the main pain points. A systematic mapping was needed to understand what was causing dissatisfaction and to prioritize improvements based on evidence.

The challenge was to turn hundreds of scattered feedback entries into actionable insights that would guide the product roadmap and unblock decisions that had been stalled for months for lack of hard data.

Methodology

I ran an in-app survey for one month and collected 245 feedback entries from healthcare professionals. The analysis combined them with CSAT data and Nielsen’s heuristics. I used positive/negative classification, semantic reading, thematic deep dives and ICE prioritization.

The goal was to structure the evidence and create a solid base for future decisions, always focused on users’ real needs.

  1. CollectionA 1-month in-app survey plus CSAT data.
  2. ClassificationPositive, negative and neutral.
  3. AnalysisSemantic reading and Nielsen’s heuristics.
  4. Deep diveA closer look at each theme.
  5. InsightsICE prioritization.
  6. DeliveryA reference document for the squads.

Main pain points

Complaints clustered around four areas: usability, complexity, performance and missing functionality.

Percentage of all 245 entries from the in-app survey (1 month). A single entry can mention more than one problem, so the total exceeds 100%.

Severity of each pain point

Severity on Nielsen’s 0–4 scale, which combines frequency, impact on the appointment and persistence. 4: blocks the work or causes data loss. 3: slows the appointment down or forces rework. 2: causes friction but has a workaround.

Lost migrated data

Records from the old system weren’t transferred correctly. Patient histories were lost.

4 · Catastrophic
Performance and freezes

Slow to save or load data. Frequent freezes that caused important information to be lost.

4 · Catastrophic
Confusing layout and interface

Cluttered layout with too much information. “Not intuitive, the information is disorganized.”

3 · Major
Too many unnecessary fields

Unneeded fields, especially for psychologists, and no custom fields for specific notes.

3 · Major
Inappropriate requirements

Mandatory ICD codes that don’t apply to psychology. Required fields that are irrelevant to the context.

2 · Minor
Filling in during the session

Complaints about having to fill in the record during the appointment, and issues switching between record and session.

2 · Minor

What users value

  • Practical once they get used to it
  • History of previous appointments
  • A useful summary field
  • Early AI integration
  • Easy repeat appointments

These positives helped shape backlog adjustments, built confidence for future changes and showed that not everything had to be rethought from scratch.

Impact

Three squads cited the research as a reference for their backlogs, and it became the foundation for the AI component and the medical record review. The document helped unblock decisions that had been stalled for months for lack of real evidence.

What I learned

This project showed the value of turning scattered feedback into actionable insights. Classification and ICE prioritization were key to turning complaints into concrete improvement opportunities.

Involving several squads in validating the findings made sure the evidence was accepted across the organization and actually made it into the product roadmap.
