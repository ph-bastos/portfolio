AI-powered care assistant

A floating component designed to bring AI features, chat and support together inside the medical record.

Design SystemAIMobile designUX ResearchPrototyping

Context and challenge

The medical record system had a complex, fragmented interface. Clinicians had to move between several sections to reach AI features, patient chat and technical support, and that scattering hurt their productivity during appointments.

The main challenge was to bring these features together without disrupting the core medical record flow, so the solution could evolve independently from the legacy system.

Solution

I designed a floating component that adapts to the appointment context and gives quick access to the most-used features. It was built to be modular and scalable.

  • A contextual interface that adapts to the appointment type
  • Quick access to AI suggestions based on the patient’s history
  • Built-in chat with non-intrusive notifications
  • Technical support with automatic prioritization
  • Responsive design for tablets and desktops
  • An independent architecture for gradual evolution

Design process

  1. Research and discoveryI interviewed 12 healthcare professionals to understand friction points in the current flow and found that 78% of their time was lost moving between sections.
  2. Ideation and conceptI explored approaches ranging from fixed sidebars to modal overlays. Usability tests showed the floating component was the most efficient option.
  3. Prototyping and validationI built interactive prototypes in Figma and ran test sessions with real users. We iterated 4 times before reaching the final version.

Results and impact

45%Shorter average appointment time
92%Clinician satisfaction rate
60%More use of AI features

The gradual rollout let us validate the solution without affecting the main system. The component is now being extended to other areas of the medical record, confirming the modular architecture.

What I learned

This project reinforced the value of incremental solutions in complex systems. The modular approach made implementation easier and allowed continuous testing and adjustments based on real user feedback.

Working closely with developers from day one was key to keeping the design technically feasible, especially given the limits of the legacy system.
